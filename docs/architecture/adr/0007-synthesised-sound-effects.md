# 0007. Synthesise feedback sounds with the Web Audio API

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-11-08

## Context and problem statement

The app needs audible feedback for a correct answer, a wrong answer and a
revealed answer. Sounds help a child who cannot read yet understand the result
without looking at the screen.

Any audio must fit the constraints: no build step, no third-party runtime
requests, MIT-compatible licensing, and the app should work offline. An
intermediate version also had background music, which was removed.

## Considered options

### Option A: Web Audio API oscillators

Pros: no files, no licence question, no download, exact control over tone and
duration, cannot fail to load, works offline.

Cons: sounds are artificial; requires a user gesture to unlock audio on most
browsers; needs care to avoid harsh tones.

### Option B: Bundled audio files

Pros: high-quality sound design, music possible.

Cons: binary assets in the repository, licensing to verify and document,
download cost, and several browsers require a gesture anyway.

### Option C: Streamed music from Free Music Archive or similar

Pros: richer experience, no repository weight.

Cons: runtime third-party request, per-track attribution obligations, and
licences (often CC BY-NC or CC BY-ND) that restrict derivative or commercial
use; it also makes the app depend on an external service.

### Option D: No audio at all

Pros: simplest, smallest.

Cons: loses a useful feedback channel for pre-literate children.

## Decision outcome

We chose **Option A**. Three short tones are generated by an oscillator with an
exponential gain envelope: a rising two-note chime for correct answers, a low
triangle for wrong answers, and a falling two-note figure for the revealed
answer. An `AudioContext` is created lazily on the first user gesture and is
reused.

Background music was implemented with the same synthesiser in an intermediate
version and then removed to keep the experience calm and the code small. There
is no music in the current version.

### Consequences

* Positive: zero audio assets, zero licensing obligations, zero downloads, and
  sounds work offline.
* Positive: the sound toggle is one boolean with one `localStorage` key.
* Negative / risks: tones sound synthetic; a musician would do better with
  recordings, at the cost of the licensing and asset burden.
* Negative / risks: browsers with audio autoplay restrictions silence the first
  interaction if the gesture is not registered. Mitigated by creating the
  context inside the click handler.
* Negative / risks: synthesised tones can be unpleasant at high volume; gains
  are kept below 0.05 to stay gentle.
